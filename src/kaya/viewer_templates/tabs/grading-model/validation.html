Scaling from 2 gyms to 6 improved everything

Going from a minimal 2-gym validation set to the full 6-gym connected network didn't just add data — it visibly tightened every estimate, especially the gym-level correction, which is hard to pin down with only two gyms to compare.

The per-gym correction's uncertainty shrinks roughly 5× — with only two gyms there's almost no way to tell "these two happen to differ by X" apart from "typical gym-to-gym spread is X." Six gyms actually constrains the population-level spread.

Does the model's own output look like the real data?

A posterior predictive check — simulate grades from the fitted model and compare to what climbers actually logged — matched closely: mean grade 5.257 (real) vs. 5.258 (simulated), per-gym means within 0.01–0.03 of each other. One minor, understood artifact: the simulation has a thin, rare low tail the real data doesn't, from two independent noise sources occasionally compounding — invisible at the bulk-of-distribution scale, not a structural problem.
