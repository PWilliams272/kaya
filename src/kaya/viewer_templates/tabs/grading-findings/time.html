How fast climbers improve

This is not a curiosity on the side — it is a threat to the gym result. The model treats a climber as a fixed quantity: there is no \(t\) anywhere in \(C_{u,g}\). So if someone logged at one gym in 2022 and another in 2025, three years of getting better is charged to the second gym. The model has nowhere else to put it.

Advancement is a steady rate that falls with grade — about +0.28 grades/year at V1, +0.04 at V9 Measured

Fitted across every watching period at once rather than read off a single window. The rate is positive and decreasing all the way up, and the top bin is consistent with zero rather than negative (−0.05 ± 0.13 at V11).

The obvious estimator is broken by regression to the mean, and says V11 climbers lose grades Rejected

Take each climber’s first and last recorded max, subtract, and divide by the years between: that estimator claims the strongest climbers lose about 1.0 grades a year. They do not. Being sorted into the V11 bin requires a high first reading, and a high reading is partly luck — so the same climber’s next reading is lower on average whether or not they improved. The rate above is fitted across all windows precisely because that removes the sorting.

Date and gym correction really do correlate, and the mechanism is not settled Open

Gyms whose data is more recent get stiffer corrections (+0.61). Climber advancement is the obvious explanation and it is not sufficient — applied as a fixed offset it accounts for only about 8% of the correlation. Within a single operating company the correlation is +0.34, so brand composition explains part of the rest, and something else explains the remainder. This is the largest open question on the page, and it is stated rather than smoothed over.
