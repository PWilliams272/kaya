What height and arm span actually buy

Candidate height shapes are compared by LOO — leave-one-out cross-validation, which scores a model by how well it predicts each observation with that observation held out. The score is elpd: total log-probability for data the model did not see. Higher is better, and only differences between models mean anything — the absolute number has no scale of its own.

Height matters, at two to three standard errors — a real effect, not an overwhelming one Real, small

Among climbers with three or more sends, adding a height term is worth about +25 ± 9 elpd, and +17 to +22 ± 8 once each climber’s personal offset is integrated out. Two identical refits of that column agree to within 0.4 (+25.4 and +25.0), which is what makes the number trustworthy rather than the size of it.

Which shape height takes is not settled — and the curved gender-split form is among the weakest Open

The saturating curve sits near the bottom in one version of the model and at the top in the other. That swing is about 1.5× its own error, so the two are not in contradiction — they simply do not pin the shape down. More awkwardly, the curved gender-split form used elsewhere in this work fails to beat no height term at all in three of four columns once the offsets are integrated out. The headline model should most likely be a straight line. Reporting this rather than quietly picking the winner is the point: the honest answer is that the data constrains how much height is worth far better than it constrains what shape the relationship has.
