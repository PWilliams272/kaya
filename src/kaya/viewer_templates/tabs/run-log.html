What is running right now

A fit occupies one core per chain for its whole life, so four chains means four cores and this machine runs two fits at a time. Three at a time was measured and it is not a mild penalty: 370 minutes each against about 85 for the same model run two at a time.

What happens next

In dependency order. Nothing downstream of an open question is queued — if a step can invalidate the one after it, the one after it does not get built until the answer is in.

What it has cost so far

The converged count is the honest one and it is small on purpose: a fit only counts if R-hat — which compares variance between chains to variance within them, where 1.00 is perfect and lower is better — came in at or under 1.01, and the effective sample size — how many genuinely independent draws the chains produced, where higher is better — cleared 400. Most of the compute on this page bought a diagnosis rather than a result.

The campaigns, and what each settled

Each campaign asked one question. Four of them answered it by ruling something out, which is the outcome that is easiest to stop recording and most expensive to rediscover.

Answers that needed no sampler

Not every question needs a fit, and the ones that don't tend to be answered faster and more convincingly. These came out of the same identity the model rests on: for a climber who logs at two gyms, subtracting their two grades cancels the climber completely, leaving a statement about the two gyms that can be measured directly. No sampler, no priors, no convergence to worry about.

Every fit, and where it is saved

Newest first. At depth cap is the share of iterations where the sampler ran out of trajectory instead of finishing one — lower is better, and it is the diagnostic that made the difference here, because it is invisible to both R-hat and effective sample size. A run can sit at 100% with zero divergences and look clean.

Traces live in runs/traces/ and results in runs/results/, both gitignored — 25 GB of NetCDF does not belong in a repository. The table lists the filename; the directory is the same for every row.

elpd (expected log pointwise predictive density) is the cross-validation score — higher is better. Its unit is not the same across this table: fits that integrate out every climber offset are scored per climber, because doing so makes that climber's rows conditionally dependent and leave-one-row-out no longer exists in closed form. The two units are not comparable to each other, which is why the column says which one it is.
